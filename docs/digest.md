- **Under a unified self-play + MCTS pipeline on Connect Four (solved, exact game-theoretic...** · score 0.020
  - _Contributions:_ The paper shows that vanilla AlphaZero achieves strong play in both Connect Four and Chomp but fails to preserve the exact trajectories required for optimal play (losing the optimal line in Connect Four; failing to consistently restore the Grundy g=0 invariant in Chomp), and that naively adding multi-frame inputs does not close this gap on Chomp. They introduce AZAL, which injects oracle-derived policy supervision as an auxiliary loss, and show it substantially improves oracle-consistency: near-perfect on 10x11 Chomp, high but incomplete on 9x10 Chomp, and improved (but still incomplete) oracle-match / delayed first mistake on Connect Four.
  - _Applicable to our work:_ Directly relevant to our verifier-in-the-loop / structural-validity concerns: this paper is empirical evidence that self-play + MCTS alone (analogous to our best-of-N sampling against a frozen verifier) reliably reaches 'good' programs but plateaus short of consistently optimal/valid trajectories, and that adding an auxiliary oracle-derived supervision signal (here, oracle policy labels; for us, verifier pass/fail or quality-metric feedback) on top of standard training closes much of that gap. Suggests hexgen's imitation-learning objective could be augmented with an AZAL-style auxiliary loss derived from the frozen mesh verifier's pass/fail and quality signals, rather than relying solely on best-of-N rejection at inference time.
  - _Additional context:_ Both test domains are small, fully oracle-evaluable combinatorial games, far simpler than CAD/mesh generation, so the magnitude of the effect may not transfer directly; still, the core finding (self-play plateaus below oracle-optimal without auxiliary oracle supervision) is a useful cautionary/motivating data point for relying purely on best-of-N + frozen verifier at inference without an auxiliary training-time signal. Only the abstract was available for extraction.
