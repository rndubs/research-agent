0.093 HiFi-BRep is a two-stage VAE + latent-diffusion pipeline for B-Rep generation. The topo...
Contributions
The paper proposes HiFi-BRep, a framework addressing both representation and generation brittleness in B-Rep synthesis. It introduces a compact B-Rep representation (Bezier curves/surfaces plus an explicit edge-face incidence matrix) and a topology-aware encoder that eliminates padding noise via learnable queries and prevents feature contamination via topology-guided (Topo-Mask) attention, yielding a high-fidelity fixed-length latent. It also designs a single-stage decoder that jointly predicts geometry and topology in parallel and enforces the manifold 'two faces per edge' constraint via a differentiable row-wise two-peak objective, rather than deferring validity to post-processing. Experiments show state-of-the-art structural validity and geometric fidelity versus BRepGen, BrepDiff, and DTGBrepGen, plus faster inference.
Applicable to our work
Highly applicable to hexgen's B-rep encoder and structural-validity goals: (1) the Topo-Mask idea -- restricting cross-entity attention to only topologically adjacent face/edge pairs via a hard attention mask -- is directly transferable to hexgen's graph-transformer encoder over the B-rep face-adjacency graph, potentially sharpening pointer/copy-head addressing by preventing irrelevant cross-face attention; (2) the row-wise two-peak differentiable-validity-objective pattern (turning a hard combinatorial validity rule -- 'each edge has exactly 2 faces' -- into a supervised, differentiable training signal rather than a post-hoc filter) is a template for embedding some of hexgen's own frozen-verifier checks (e.g. simpler structural ones like feature-edge capture or element-budget sanity) directly into training rather than relying solely on best-of-N + reject; (3) their single-stage parallel joint geometry+topology decoding vs. cascaded generation ablation (Valid 95.2%->69.3% when reverting to cascaded) is direct evidence that jointly generating structure and parameters (as hexgen's pointer/coordinate-vocabulary decoder already does) outperforms sequential decomposition, reinforcing hexgen's existing single-decoder design choice.
Additional context
This is the closest paper of the four to hexgen's actual domain (CAD B-Rep generation) and the highest-confidence match. Code and pretrained models are public (github.com/1nnoh/HiFi-BRep), so architectural details (Topo-Mask implementation, row-wise two-peak loss) could be inspected directly rather than re-derived from the paper text. Note their B-reps are unconditional/class-conditional generative targets (~6-29 faces, similar scale to hexgen's 7-15 faces) rather than a program/DSL output, so this bears on the ENCODER and validity-loss side of hexgen, not the decoder's program-generation grammar.

0.067 TDD-Agent is a single agent operationalizing test-driven development: Phase 1 (test-fir...
Contributions
The paper proposes TDD-Agent, which adapts test-driven development to LLM code generation by treating test generation as a reasoning step performed before implementation rather than a fixed post-hoc validator. It shows on LiveCodeBench (via a lightweight TDD-prompt variant) that test-first reasoning alone consistently improves over CoT/SCoT/Self-Planning/ICoT baselines. On the repository-level RepoEval benchmark, the full TDD-Agent framework, which additionally allows tests themselves to be revised jointly with code across iterations, consistently outperforms retrieval-based (RAG, RepoCoder) and agent-based (mini-SWE-agent) baselines.
Applicable to our work
The dual-track refinement loop -- iteratively revising BOTH the generated artifact and its own self-check (here: tests; for hexgen: candidate program) using execution/verifier feedback, rather than treating the verifier as a fixed post-hoc filter -- is a directly transferable pattern for repair/edit distillation (care area 1): after a best-of-N candidate program fails the frozen verifier, feed the specific verifier failure signal (e.g. which check failed: compiles / inverted elements / Jacobian bound / surface fidelity) back to the model for targeted repair, and track iteration-level improvement the way this paper tracks pass-rate-per-iteration, rather than only doing flat resampling.
Additional context
Domain is software/code generation, not geometry, and the 'test' side of the dual-track loop has no clean hexgen analogue (our verifier is frozen and machine-checkable, not something the model generates and can revise) -- so only the code-repair-via-execution-feedback half transfers directly, not the co-evolving-oracle half. Also worth noting: the paper's own failure analysis (matched failures = self-consistent but wrong) is a caution against letting a model-generated proxy substitute for the real frozen verifier, which reinforces hexgen's existing design of keeping the verifier frozen and external.

Contributions
The paper presents a two-phase architecture separating task-directed visual state acquisition from goal-directed symbolic planning through a narrow grounded-state interface. It introduces a state-dependent decoding procedure that enforces PDDL action applicability at the token level, coupled with language-guided MCTS over executable plan prefixes, guaranteeing plans are executable by construction under the transition model. Applicable to our work
The state-dependent constrained decoding + MCTS-over-executable-prefixes pattern is close to directly transferable to hexgen: mask the decoder's token distribution at each step to only tokens/pointer targets that keep the partial hex-meshing program compileable/executable under the deterministic mesher's grammar (analogous to PDDL applicability), then use token/step-level MCTS with PUCB (LM prior + a value from partial verifier checks, e.g. partial Jacobian/element-budget feasibility) to search over executable program prefixes rather than relying on flat best-of-N sampling after the fact. Their component ablation (Table 2, constraints-alone vs. search-alone vs. combined) is a template for validating whether constrained decoding or MCTS (or both) is where hexgen's structural-validity gains actually come from.
Additional context
Domain is household robot planning (PDDL/VirtualHome/ALFWorld), not CAD, so transfer is purely at the decoding/search-architecture level. Note the validity guarantee is conditional on domain-model soundness and correct instance grounding -- for hexgen the analogous caveat is that constrained decoding only guarantees syntactic/grammar validity, not full verifier pass (quality bounds, surface-match fidelity still need best-of-N/MCTS value guidance as this paper does with its planning heuristic). 0.039 3D CAD consensus selection: sample N parametric CAD programs from an LLM, compile each...
Contributions
The paper proposes verifier-free 'consensus selection' for text-to-CAD generation: instead of using a separate vision-language judge, it compiles every sampled candidate program to a 3D model and picks the one that agrees most with the rest of the pool, under geometric or topological notions of agreement. On the candidate pools of a state-of-the-art CAD generation method, geometric consensus improves all three geometric metrics over that method's own verifier, and topological consensus matches it on topology. The method is training-free and works across every tested LLM and prompt variant, reducing Chamfer distance by 1-10% versus random selection from the same pool.
Applicable to our work
Directly applicable to hexgen's best-of-N candidate screening: alongside (or as a cheap pre-filter before) the frozen machine-checkable verifier, a consensus/agreement score computed across the compiled meshes of the N sampled hex-meshing programs could re-rank or triage candidates using signal the pool itself provides, potentially reducing how often the expensive verifier must run.
Additional context
Consensus selection implicitly assumes the majority of sampled candidates are 'roughly correct', so the informative signal is majority agreement; if hexgen's decoder occasionally produces a correct-but-structurally-distinct minority candidate (e.g. a valid but unusual block/sweep decomposition), consensus selection could systematically penalize good outliers -- worth checking against hexgen's actual candidate-pool diversity before adopting wholesale.

0.027 Introduces 'consilience', a verifier-free rollout-selection metric that scores each rea...
Contributions
The paper shows confidence-based verifier-free test-time scaling catastrophically breaks down on hard problems because uniformly high confidence often marks a failure to explore rather than correctness. It introduces consilience, a metric that evaluates the temporal asymmetry of a rollout's confidence trajectory (low-then-high) to pick better completions without any external verifier. Experiments on graduate math, QA, and free-form code generation show consilience beats confidence-maximization and matches or exceeds verifier-based selection in some settings. It notably works in domains like free-form code generation where voting-based selection is inapplicable.
Applicable to our work
Hexgen screens best-of-N candidate programs with an expensive frozen machine-checkable verifier. Consilience's confidence-trajectory signal could serve as a cheap, verifier-free pre-filter or complementary ranking over decoder rollouts -- e.g. to triage which of N sampled programs to run through the verifier first under a compute budget, or as an additional selection signal alongside the verifier's pass/fail result.
Additional context
Validated only on free-form LLM token-level reasoning (math/code/QA); hexgen's decoder emits short DSL programs via pointer/copy heads over a small closed vocabulary, a very different confidence regime, so it's unclear the same low-then-high confidence signature would emerge -- would need empirical validation on hexgen's own rollouts before relying on it to replace/triage the frozen verifier.

0.026 Hypothesis Frontier is a verifier-guided neurosymbolic search loop: each LLM-proposed f...
Contributions
The paper introduces Hypothesis Frontier, which evaluates every LLM-generated first-order formula exactly against training data, uses the concrete errors to drive bounded symbolic repair of the LLM's own formula, and retains the strongest verified formula across LLM rounds rather than treating each generation as final. It also proposes a final exact-simplification pass that shortens train-valid formulas without changing any training prediction, and a symbolic-first cascade that runs a Z3 solver before invoking the LLM loop at all. Under matched models and LLM-round budgets it beats repeated direct prompting in 9 of 9 comparisons.
Applicable to our work
The core loop — when a best-of-N candidate fails our frozen verifier, don't discard it; extract the concrete residual (which check failed: inverted elements, Jacobian/aspect-ratio bound, surface-fidelity mismatch, feature-edge miss) and use it to drive a bounded, parent-derived repair of the model's own DSL program (edit/patch the offending block/sweep/projection call rather than resample from scratch), then keep a deterministic 'best verified-so-far' frontier program across repair rounds and feed its residual failures back as context for the next generation attempt — this maps directly onto our structural-validity and repair/edit-distillation care area. The 'symbolic-first cascade' (run a cheap deterministic procedure first, only invoke the learned model on what it can't resolve) also echoes our existing deterministic-construction-template auto-labeling path and suggests we could similarly gate hexgen invocation behind template-based solvers for the parts they already cover.
Additional context
The domain (first-order logic formula induction with a trivial finite-model verifier) is far cheaper to check than our mesh verifier (compiling + meshing + Jacobian/aspect-ratio computation), so a per-round repair loop of this style would be much more expensive for us per iteration; the paper's own ablation shows repair reduces mismatch on 83-98% of invalid candidates but reaches exact validity immediately only 1-8% of the time, which is a useful expectation-setter for how much a similar repair loop might realistically buy us rather than a guarantee of large gains. 0.021 RISTER is an encoder-decoder STR network combining a rotation-equivariant encoder (RELG...
Contributions
The paper proves that the cross-attention mechanism is rotation-invariant when its query is held fixed and its source (key/value) tokens are rotated, and uses this to derive design principles for a Rotation-Invariant Text Decoder (RITD). It also proposes RELG, a rotation-equivariant local-global backbone combining deep equivariant convolutions with self-attention to preserve fine-grained detail and model inter-character dependencies. Combining RELG and RITD yields RISTER, an end-to-end rotation-invariant scene text recognizer that needs no orientation-estimation module or rotation-based data augmentation.
Applicable to our work
Our own 'make the encoder natively rotation-invariant' line is closed, but this paper's most transferable idea is decoder-side and architecture-agnostic: constructing the decoder's query purely from already-emitted output tokens (not from the raw, possibly-rotated input features) makes any subsequent cross-/pointer-attention over the input features provably invariant to whatever transformation was applied to those features. Applied to our pointer/copy decoder over B-rep faces, this suggests forming the pointer query solely from the previously-emitted program tokens (never mixing in raw per-face coordinate features into the query itself) so that the face-addressing/copy attention becomes provably rotation-invariant regardless of how the B-rep's face features are rotated — a decoder-only change that doesn't require revisiting the (closed) equivariant-encoder question. The underlying proof technique (cross-attention over an unordered token set is invariant to any joint transform of keys/values given an invariant query) generalizes beyond 2D images to graphs/sets in general.
Additional context
Because our 'encoder rotation-invariance' line is explicitly closed per the problem statement, most of this paper's direct architectural contribution (the RELG equivariant-convolution backbone, tied to a discrete C4 group over 2D pixel grids) has limited transfer value and does not obviously port to 3D SE(3) B-rep face graphs, which are non-grid and variable-cardinality. The decoder-side invariant-query trick is the one genuinely new, cheaply-testable mechanism, and it is worth flagging that RISTER's guarantee is exact only at 4 canonical angles and approximate elsewhere — our own rotation robustness needs are likely continuous (arbitrary part orientation), so the guarantee would only partially transfer.

Contributions
The paper proposes explicitly modeling the evolving world state and delegating exact geometric computation to a fixed, zero-parameter renderer while letting only the neural model handle appearance, instantiated as Marionette for articulated multi-character games. It shows the predicted state is directly controllable (forcing a mismatched action stream changes joint error by 31%) and that long-horizon failures (character drift, ground penetration) are properties of the state that can be repaired by rules imposed on the state alone, without touching the observation model. Routing appearance through the predicted state costs no detectable fidelity versus an end-to-end pixel-autoregressive baseline.
Applicable to our work
The core offloading principle -- predict a small, structured, interpretable state with a neural model and delegate all exact geometric computation to a fixed, zero-parameter deterministic operator -- is structurally identical to hexgen's split between a neural program-emitting decoder and a deterministic mesher executor. 0.009 The authors prove that for a given feature space and input symmetry group, the increase...
Contributions
The paper gives a rigorous mathematical characterization of 'symmetry increase' in equivariant neural networks: it proves the increased symmetry has a computable infimum fixed by the feature space, derives an orbit-type-based algorithm and design guidelines to reach that infimum, and shows under regularity assumptions that generic equivariant maps attain it. Experiments on synthetic k-fold-symmetric structures and the real-world QM9 dataset validate the theoretical predictions and show the guidelines reduce harmful representation collapse.
Applicable to our work
Our B-rep face graphs frequently contain congruent/symmetric substructures (e.g. repeated faces from patterned or symmetric parts), which is exactly the setting where pointer/copy heads face orbit/symmetry ambiguity when addressing faces. This paper's symmetry-infimum framework and feature-design guidelines are a genuinely new (not our closed rotation-invariant-encoder) mechanism for reasoning about and reducing harmful symmetry collapse in our graph-transformer encoder's features, which could directly inform how we design pointer/copy addressing to avoid ambiguous ties between congruent faces.
Additional context
Highly theoretical (group-representation-theory heavy); applying it to our setting would require translating the orbit-type analysis from continuous SO(3)/O(3) symmetry groups to our discrete/mixed graph-symmetry setting (permutation symmetry among congruent faces), which is nontrivial. Code released at the linked GitHub repo, which may help validate applicability faster than re-deriving the theory from scratch.

Contributions

Applicable to our work
