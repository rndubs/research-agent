Contributions
The paper presents Discovery Loop, a minimal, open-source, single-machine reimplementation of the AlphaEvolve-style LLM-guided program evolution loop that uses one LLM call per iteration with no distributed infrastructure. Applied to the Packomania csqv circle-packing benchmark, it produced new best-known solutions for 10 of 12 targets (N=101-114), improving on prior records by 2.4%-5.4%, independently verified and accepted by Packomania, at a total LLM cost of $27.72 over 15 iterations in about 8 hours on a consumer PC. The authors also analyze cost-efficiency dynamics across iterations and propose a plateau-detection mechanism that would have cut cost by 50% with negligible quality loss, and catalog several non-trivial algorithmic ideas (e.g. hexagonal lattice initialization, defect migration) the LLM discovered on its own.
Applicable to our work
The core loop — LLM proposes a full candidate program, an independent zero-tolerance (no-shared-code) verifier checks it, only verified improvements are kept, with a scoreboard/history fed back into the next prompt — is structurally analogous to our best-of-N + frozen-verifier screening setup (problem 6, self-play/verifier-in-the-loop training) and to problem 1 (structural validity via execution/verifier-guided generation), though here the 'decoder' is an LLM prompted in natural language rather than our trained graph-transformer. The 'full replacement over patch' design choice and the per-target-best-tracking-separate-from-champion idea could inform how we structure rejection-sampled SFT rounds or best-of-N bookkeeping across many parts. The plateau-detection mechanism (windowed diminishing-returns stopping) is a directly transferable, cheap idea for deciding when to stop a best-of-N sampling budget or a self-play round for a given part/target.
Additional context
This is an LLM-agent (prompted-program-search) paper, not a trained neural decoder, so the applicability is at the level of loop design and verifier discipline rather than architecture. Results (circle packing record-breaking) are in a very different domain (continuous geometric optimization via classical numerical methods) and the 'verifier' here is a simple feasibility/overlap checker, much simpler than our scaled-Jacobian / surface-fidelity verifier — the core principle (independent, zero-tolerance, no-shared-code checker) is the transferable piece. Small-scale, single-author, non-peer-reviewed-venue paper (practicalsystems.io); treat headline numbers as self-reported though independently accepted by a third-party database (Packomania), which adds some credibility. The cost/iteration economics (specific dollar amounts) are not generalizable to our setting since they depend on LLM-as-agent pricing, not model training cost.

The paper proves that mixed training (synthetic and real data combined throughout SGD) induces strong model collapse -- a persistent excess-risk floor -- while two-stage training, which restricts synthetic data to an initial stage, avoids this floor entirely, showing collapse is not inevitable under a simple data curriculum. It further derives scaling laws under a random sketch model showing larger models can amplify synthetic-induced degradation under mixing, quantifies how high-quality synthetic pretraining can reduce bias in two-stage training, and gives an exact finite-sample condition for two-stage training to strictly outperform real-data-only training.
Applicable to our work

The paper proposes a verifier-guided explainable reasoning framework combining gold-anchored QLoRA fine-tuning, task-aware routing to external symbolic verifiers (FOL/Z3 for logic, a unit-aware solver for physics) instead of a neural mixture-of-experts, and a group-relative RLVR scheme whose reward separately scores answer correctness (P1), evidence/unit consistency (P2), and reasoning depth (P3). Applicable to our work
Highly applicable as a training-pipeline template for hexgen's structural-validity and verifier-in-the-loop goals: (1) the task-aware router to an external, deterministic, machine-checkable verifier (FOL/Z3 for logic, unit-aware solver for physics) is structurally identical to dispatching a generated hexgen program to our frozen verifier (compiles / zero inverted elements / Jacobian and aspect-ratio bounds / surface fidelity / feature-edge capture / element-budget sanity) for a specific pass/fail breakdown rather than one opaque accept/reject signal. Contributions
The paper identifies a spurious-advantage failure mode in the GRPO advantage estimator that rewards lucky guesses as much as genuine reasoning whenever a task's effective answer surface is finite. Applicable to our work
