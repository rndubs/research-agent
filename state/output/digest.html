0.025 CudaPerf is a reflective RL framework combining verifiable execution rewards with struc...
Contributions
The paper proposes CudaPerf, a reflective reinforcement-learning framework for CUDA kernel generation that augments verifiable execution rewards with structural, parallelization-aware code rewards, trained in an offline contrastive-ranking stage followed by online RL with iterative execution-feedback refinement. It also introduces a dataset of 2.9k C-to-CUDA and 1k PyTorch-to-CUDA programs with diverse implementations and input configurations, and shows CudaPerf substantially outperforms strong baselines (Qwen-3-32B, CUDA Agent) in both speedup and correctness.
Applicable to our work
The two-stage recipe -- offline contrastive pairwise ranking of program candidates followed by online RL with a unified reward combining execution/structural-validity signals, plus iterative execution-feedback refinement of the model's own broken rollouts -- maps closely onto our best-of-N plus frozen-verifier setup: our verifier's compile/valid-mesh/quality-bound checks could serve as the structural and execution reward terms for an analogous RL or DAgger-style repair-distillation loop on hexgen's own rollouts.
Additional context
Domain is CUDA kernel code generation rather than CAD/mesh DSL programs, but both are settings where autoregressive decoders must emit programs that both execute correctly and satisfy secondary structural-quality properties beyond bare validity -- the reward-decomposition approach (execution correctness + structural/quality-aware terms) is a close conceptual match to our verifier's multi-criteria checks (Jacobian/aspect-ratio quality, feature-edge capture, element-budget sanity).

0.022 Progressive Seed Pruning (PSP) scores intermediate denoised estimates and progressively...
Contributions
The paper introduces Progressive Seed Pruning (PSP), an inference-time scaling method for diffusion and flow-matching models that evaluates many noise seeds early and progressively narrows the candidate set based on intermediate denoised estimates, using a fixed compute budget more effectively than existing best-of-N or resampling approaches. Across diffusion and flow-matching backbones, PSP improves reward-guided selection and achieves higher automated (GenEval) and human-evaluated prompt-alignment scores than best-of-N, importance-sampling, and tree-search baselines at matched compute.
Applicable to our work
The progressive-pruning-under-fixed-budget mechanism is transferable to our best-of-N program screening against the frozen verifier: score partial/intermediate decoder rollouts and prune low-promise candidates early instead of fully executing/verifying every candidate to completion, evaluating more candidates within the same verifier-call budget.
Additional context
Domain is image diffusion/flow-matching, not autoregressive program synthesis, so the scoring signal (denoised-estimate quality) would need to be replaced by a partial-program value estimate or fast proxy verifier; the core budget-allocation idea (front-load exploration, prune progressively) is architecture-agnostic.

0.017 Diagnostic analysis of LDT on clue-rich Sudoku: measures how much of the grid is commit...
Contributions
The paper shows that a neural Sudoku solver marketed as performing branching search actually commits nearly the whole solution in a single forward pass and is functionally a one-shot predictor wrapped in an exact verifier; all failures trace to a single early, confident wrong deletion ('first-pass poisoning') rather than to search quality. It further shows that adding learned search machinery (CoLT) does not change which instances are solved, only how much wasted invalid-derivation compute is cut, and that two calibration/symmetry interventions -- digit-permutation augmentation and test-time union over symmetry-transformed passes -- dramatically raise accuracy without retraining. On an out-of-distribution graph-coloring task, the one-shot behavior disappears and search actually changes outcomes, delimiting where the diagnosis applies.
Applicable to our work
The core diagnostic question -- is this verifier-screened autoregressive model actually doing search, or is it a one-shot predictor whose apparent best-of-N selection just amortizes a single confident (and sometimes wrong) forward pass? -- is directly analogous to hexgen's best-of-N-plus-frozen-verifier pipeline, and worth running as a sanity check (does hexgen's decoder 'first-pass poison' by locking in a wrong face/coordinate choice early, with the frozen verifier just filtering symptoms rather than the model doing real structural search?). Two concrete, low-cost transferable mechanisms: (1) test-time union over symmetry-transformed passes (run the encoder-decoder on several rotated/permuted views of the B-rep and union/vote the candidate programs before verifier screening) as a deployment-time robustness trick for the rotation/pose-robustness care area; (2) a permutation/relabeling-style data augmentation (analogous to digit-permutation here) over face/coordinate labels to break spurious ordering shortcuts, relevant to the domain-gap/tokenization-mismatch care area.
Additional context
Results are on toy combinatorial puzzles (Sudoku, graph coloring), not CAD/geometry, so the specific numbers don't transfer -- but the underlying methodology (probe how much of the output a single forward pass commits vs how much search actually contributes, then intervene with symmetry augmentation) is a cheap, generalizable diagnostic worth adapting to hexgen's own verifier-in-the-loop pipeline before investing further in search machinery. 0.008 A two-stage diffusion framework that decouples topology and geometry: a surface-centric...
Contributions
The paper proposes TG-Diff, a lightweight two-stage diffusion-based B-rep generation framework that decouples topology modeling from geometric modeling via a surface-centric representation (surfaces + adjacency only). Two independent diffusion models generate surface adjacency (via discrete diffusion/D3PM) and surface latents (via a topology-conditioned latent diffusion DiT), with topology used as guidance to stabilize and structurally complete the geometry generation. Edges/vertices are derived by post-processing the decoded surfaces into a watertight B-rep. Despite a compact footprint (82.18M parameters, 2.2 GFLOPs), the method reports superior validity and COV/MMD/JSD metrics versus prior work on DeepCAD and ABC.
Applicable to our work
The core idea -- decoupling topology (a small discrete adjacency/graph structure) from continuous geometry generation, and using the generated topology to condition/guide geometry generation -- is directly analogous to hexgen's face-graph-conditioned program generation and could inform a two-pass generation strategy (predict block/sweep structure/connectivity first, then condition coordinate/parameter generation on that structure) to improve structural validity. Their post-hoc derivation of edges/vertices from decoded surfaces (deterministic reconstruction from a smaller committed representation) is also analogous to hexgen's deterministic mesher executing a committed program -- worth reviewing for how they guarantee watertightness/consistency after generation, which maps to our verifier's structural checks.
Additional context
This is diffusion-based (parallel, discrete+continuous diffusion), not autoregressive/pointer-based like hexgen, so the training/inference mechanics differ substantially; the transferable idea is the topology/geometry decoupling and topology-first conditioning, not the diffusion machinery itself. Only the abstract was available for extraction (single-chunk paper text); no limitations, dataset details beyond names, or code link were stated in the available text -- extraction is grounded solely in the abstract.

0.007 Best-of-Evidence (BoE) keeps the BoN candidate pool fixed, represents reusable claims v...
Contributions
The paper introduces Best-of-Evidence (BoE), an inference-time selection framework for best-of-N candidate pools under partial verification, using a signed candidate-factor claim graph and a budget-limited evidence controller. It provides theoretical results showing residual evidence capacity bounds achievable improvement and that shared factor queries can achieve O(log K) versus Theta(K) query cost separation, and shows empirically on four medical VQA settings that BoE can improve fixed-pool selection and rescue some BoN failures.
Applicable to our work
Our verifier is a frozen set of independent partial checks (compiles, zero inverted elements, Jacobian/aspect-ratio bounds, surface-match fidelity, feature-edge capture, element-budget) rather than one whole-program verifier -- BoE's idea of representing these partial checks as a shared claim/factor graph across the N candidate programs and adaptively allocating verifier-check budget to the checks most likely to change which candidate is selected could reduce the number of full verifier runs needed during best-of-N screening.
Additional context
Application domain is medical VQA, quite far from CAD/mesh program generation, and the theoretical guarantees are stated for a 'factor-code model' abstraction whose fit to our verifier's check structure is unproven; would need validation that our verifier checks are similarly decomposable and reusable across candidates.

#1 0.058 Models the per-instance false-accept rate as a latent variable drawn from a distributio...
Contributions
The paper gives a minimal theory of partially correlated verifier cascades: modeling per-instance false-accept/true-accept rates as latent variables, it derives that posterior log-odds are concave in the number of gates k (with the independence-based Odds Law as just the tangent/upper bound), that failure can decay only polynomially instead of exponentially under correlation, and that a 'blind-spot' probability mass caps achievable reliability below 1 regardless of how many gates are added. It also shows the theory is empirically measurable and that independence-based extrapolation can underestimate failure by orders of magnitude at larger k.
Applicable to our work
Directly applicable to designing/calibrating hexgen's own frozen verifier, which is itself a multi-gate cascade (compiles; zero inverted elements; scaled-Jacobian/aspect-ratio bounds; surface-match fidelity; feature-edge capture; element-budget sanity): this theory gives a principled way to estimate how correlated those gates are, whether adding more checks is still buying reliability or hitting a blind-spot ceiling, and suggests decorrelating checks (e.g. across independent geometric criteria) rather than stacking more of the same kind of check when screening best-of-N candidates.
Additional context
This is a theoretical/methods note (builds on arXiv:2606.15712, the 'Odds Law' paper) rather than an applied CAD or program-synthesis paper; abstract-only extraction. The practical takeaway ('decorrelate, don't just add gates') is the most directly actionable piece for hexgen's verifier design.

#4 0.029 The paper proves that a broad class of hierarchical graph transformers combining global...
Contributions
The paper characterizes the expressive power of global-attention graph foundation models for MILPs, proving they are bounded by the 1-WL test regardless of parameter setting, and validates this across ten diverse encoder architectures (Graphormer-, GraphGPS-, Set-Transformer-, and Gasse-style). It further shows that expressiveness beyond 1-WL comes from input encoding rather than attention: random-walk positional encodings can separate 1-WL-equivalent instance pairs, though further constructions expose the limits of that remedy. The result yields an encoder-agnostic diagnostic for detecting 1-WL-induced representation equivalence.
Applicable to our work
hexgen's graph-transformer encoder over B-rep face-adjacency graphs (Graphormer-style edge-biased attention) could suffer the same 1-WL expressiveness ceiling, meaning distinct-but-WL-equivalent faces could be mapped to identical embeddings before the pointer/copy head addresses them -- directly bearing on the 'symmetry/orbit ambiguity when pointing at congruent nodes' care area. The paper's diagnostic could be run on hexgen's encoder to test for such collapse, and its random-walk-positional-encoding remedy is a concrete, low-cost mechanism to try if collapse is found.
Additional context
Domain studied is MILP graphs, not B-rep, but the architectural components (global linear attention, edge-weighted cross-attention, Graphormer/GraphGPS-style encoders) closely match hexgen's encoder family, so the WL-boundedness result plausibly transfers directly; only the abstract was available, so exact proof scope and remedy details are unverified.

Contributions
OPSD-V is an on-policy self-distillation scheme: a student follows its own exact inference-time AR rollout (own KV cache), while a teacher is evaluated at the same visited denoising states but with a cleaner AR-consistent cache where older history is replaced by real long-video context, providing dense denoising-level corrective targets without changing the sampler or inference-time cache mechanism.
Applicable to our work
The on-policy correction pattern (student rolls out exactly as at inference time; a teacher supplies corrective targets at the same visited states using cleaner context) is a directly transferable template for DAgger-style repair distillation on hexgen's own broken program rollouts -- e.g. having a teacher re-derive a corrective continuation from a verifier-clean program state whenever the student's own autoregressive rollout drifts, rather than training only on clean expert trajectories.

The 'cleaner-context teacher' has no direct hexgen analogue — you'd need to synthesize one (e.g. verifier-repaired continuations), which is a moderate new training-loop component rather than a drop-in.

#7 0.025 VRR-Stop uses a four-parameter noise model separating verifier false acceptance and fal...
Contributions
The paper proposes VRR-Stop, a robust stopping framework for noisy verify-repair-repeat loops in LLM agents that models verifier and repairer noise separately and decides whether to continue repairing based on the sign of the estimated marginal gain in validity. It also introduces VRR-Guard, an estimation-free fallback for when verifier discrimination is too weak for calibration to be reliable. On a GSM8K stress setting, VRR-Stop substantially improves final true validity over fixed-round repair while using fewer average repair rounds.
Applicable to our work
Directly transferable stopping/decision framework for hexgen's best-of-N plus frozen-verifier plus repair pipeline: a VRR-Stop-style noise model and belief-filtering could decide when to keep repairing a candidate hex-mesh program versus commit or discard it, and VRR-Guard's margin-based fallback guards against acting on an unreliable signal, directly targeting the structural-validity and verifier-in-the-loop-training care areas.
Additional context
hexgen's verifier is described as a FROZEN, machine-checkable, deterministic pass/fail (compiles, zero inverted elements, quality bounds, etc.), so it likely has much higher discrimination than the noisy LLM verifiers this paper targets -- the explicit noise model may be unnecessary overhead, but the general stopping-criterion framing (marginal-gain sign, cost-bounded repair, guard against low-signal regimes) remains a directly applicable design pattern for a repair/edit-distillation loop.
