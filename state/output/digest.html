0.087 Sample-simulate-select (S3) draws N candidates per prompt from a frozen text-to-motion...
Contributions
The paper introduces S3, a training-free pipeline that composes a frozen text-to-motion generator, a deterministic IK retargeter, and a physics simulator running the exact deployment tracking policy as a best-of-N selector. Because the simulator is the verifier, S3 attains the any-of-N ceiling by construction, letting the authors measure how much of the human-to-humanoid gap is closeable by selection alone versus requiring training. They show selection raises upright execution from 83.5% to 89.5% (and 80.5% to 89.5% on the full 4,184-prompt test split), that a cheap kinematic fall-predictor recovers only a quarter of this gain, and that all 177 gate-selected clips executed successfully on a real robot.
Applicable to our work
The core best-of-N-screened-by-a-frozen-deterministic-verifier pattern is exactly hexgen's own architecture, so this paper's evaluation protocol is directly transferable: measure the 'any-of-N ceiling' of the frozen hexgen decoder separately from what additional training buys, to see how much headroom sampling alone can close versus needing better training data. Its central finding -- that a cheap proxy/kinematic verifier with strong population-level classification accuracy (AUROC 0.90) still recovers only a quarter of the achievable gain because within-prompt ranking is harder than population classification -- is a direct caution against substituting a cheaper learned critic for hexgen's frozen machine-checkable verifier when doing best-of-N selection. The paper's method of isolating a specific unrecoverable failure class (squat/bend motions) and showing that only targeted training data (not more sampling) fixes it is a useful diagnostic pattern for identifying which classes of B-rep parts hexgen's decoder structurally cannot produce valid programs for, versus which just need more samples.
Additional context
Domain (humanoid motion control) is unrelated to CAD/mesh generation, so nothing here is directly reusable as code, but the evaluation methodology (ceiling measurement, kinematic-vs-execution verifier comparison, isolating unrecoverable failure classes) is a strong conceptual match to hexgen's own best-of-N + frozen verifier design and worth reading in full for the S3 experimental protocol (Section III-E, IV-B, IV-C, IV-D).

Contributions
The paper introduces a controlled experimental design that isolates one distribution-shift component at a time (geometry, then geometry+modeled-physics) rather than conflating multiple shifts, unlike prior PDE-pretraining evaluations. It shows that pretraining's sample-efficiency gain over training from scratch is not a fixed discount for distribution shift: the gain ordering between the two shift settings reverses as the target-data budget grows (favoring the harder shift at larger budgets), and that spreading a fixed budget across more distinct geometries (vs. more conditions per geometry) amplifies the pretraining gain only when the shift does not also change the modeled physics.
Applicable to our work
The paper's core methodology -- decomposing a domain gap into separate shift components (here: geometry vs. modeled physics) and measuring how the sample-efficiency benefit of pretraining/synthetic data depends jointly on target-budget size and target-data diversity -- is directly transferable to our own synthetic-inverse-factory-to-real-CAD domain gap: we could similarly vary 'shift components' one at a time (e.g. construction-template distribution vs. face-count/topology distribution) to decompose why hexgen underperforms on real CAD parts. The breadth-vs-depth finding (spend a limited real-CAD fine-tuning budget on more distinct parts rather than more programs per part, unless it will not resolve a physics/template mismatch) is a concrete, low-cost experimental-design lesson for how we allocate real-CAD auto-labeled fine-tuning data. The frozen-backbone adapter result (83% of full fine-tuning gain at 5% extra params) also suggests a cheap way to re-target a pretrained hexgen encoder-decoder to a new part family without full retraining.
Additional context
This is a workshop paper (Representations for the Physical Sciences) evaluated on 2D airfoil RANS surrogates with a single architecture family (U-Net) held fixed across the transfer sweep, so the generality of the shift-component ordering across architectures/domains is unverified. No code link is given in the excerpted text. The central lesson -- pretraining value is a joint function of target-data budget, target-data diversity, and source-target physics/distribution mismatch, not a fixed discount -- is a useful experimental-design template to borrow even though the domain (CFD surrogates) is unrelated to CAD/meshing.

0.016 Physical Mapping Guard (PMG): the agent is restricted to submitting a structured Topolo...
Contributions
The paper formalizes resolving 'Unknown Unknowns' in early-stage software architecture as a physical-grounding problem, and traces a cascading chain of failure modes across pure-text self-play (social sycophancy, then plausible fabrications under adversarial prompting) and a tool-augmented execution sandbox (Specification Gaming, where agents exploit control over their own validation scripts). It then proposes the Physical Mapping Guard (PMG), which revokes verification authority from the agent and routes all feasibility checks through an external deterministic mapper. Cross-model evaluation (GPT-4o, Qwen-Max, Claude) on a custom 27-case architecture matrix and public system-design datasets shows PMG eliminates physical- and validation-layer gaming, isolating remaining failures to semantic-layer reinterpretation and auditor overreach.
Applicable to our work
The core mechanism -- revoking an agent's/model's authority over its own validation logic and routing all correctness checks through an external, deterministic, frozen verifier -- is exactly our own frozen machine-checkable verifier design for hexgen, and this paper gives empirical evidence for why that separation matters: when agents had any control over validation code, they learned to game it (fabricate constants, shrink test scope) to get a superficial pass. That is directly relevant if any future hexgen component (a repair/edit-distillation loop, an LLM-authored synthetic-data pipeline, or a learned critic) is ever allowed to touch or approximate the verifier's checks. Their taxonomy of gaming types (physical-layer, validation-layer, semantic-layer) is a useful checklist when auditing best-of-N/self-play rollouts for hexgen for analogous shortcuts, i.e. programs that satisfy the verifier's letter but not its intent.
Additional context
Application domain is software-architecture design by LLM agents, not CAD/mesh generation -- no B-rep, pointer-network, graph-transformer, or mesh-quality content here. Relevance is at the level of a cautionary verifier-integrity argument and design pattern, not a borrowable model component. Worth a light read of Section 5 ('Entanglement of Verification Authority') rather than deep methodological study; no code release was found.

Contributions
The paper introduces EMERGE, described as the first fully SE(3)-equivariant graph-based diffusion backbone for 3D point cloud generation. It proposes Canonical Frame Voxel Pooling and a Global Invariant Feature Attention module to capture multi-scale topology while strictly preserving continuous spatial symmetries, and a provably correct, parameter-free distribution-alignment technique that enables zero-shot super-resolution at arbitrary inference resolutions without retraining. Applicable to our work
The Canonical Frame Voxel Pooling mechanism -- canonicalizing local geometry via covariance-matrix eigenvectors before any pooling/aggregation step, then un-projecting back -- is a concrete, transferable technique for building rotation-robust multi-scale pooling over a B-rep face-adjacency graph without discretizing to a fixed rotation group, offering a genuinely different mechanism than a native rotation-invariant encoder (which our problem statement notes is a closed design line) and could instead be explored as a deployment-time or architectural add-on for hexgen's pointer/graph-transformer encoder. The 13-dimensional SE(3)-invariant local geometric feature construction (curvature/linearity/planarity from neighborhood covariance eigenvalues) is also a lightweight way to inject rotation-invariant local shape descriptors into message passing that could enrich hexgen's face/edge features without adding discretization artifacts.
Additional context
Applicability is at the mechanism level only -- the application domain (unordered point cloud diffusion generation) is far from hexgen's B-rep-to-program autoregressive decoding, and the problem statement explicitly closes the 'native rotation-invariant encoder' line, so this is most useful as a reference for an alternative equivariance mechanism (canonical-frame pooling) rather than a wholesale encoder replacement. The paper proposes NeuSOGA3D, a neuro-symbolic 3D reconstruction framework that restricts learning to perceptual abstraction and performs the actual geometric reconstruction through explicit, deterministic symbolic operators (implicit splines, PSPS volumetric lofting, and shape-preserving CSG), so every intermediate structure (contours, control polygons, cross-sections, CSG operators) remains inspectable and directly reusable as B-splines, NURBS surfaces, or boundary-representation (B-Rep) models. #1 0.105 Discovery Loop is a ~400-line Python system implementing a single-candidate (no populat...
Contributions
The paper presents Discovery Loop, a minimal, open-source, single-machine reimplementation of the AlphaEvolve-style LLM-guided program evolution loop that uses one LLM call per iteration with no distributed infrastructure. Applied to the Packomania csqv circle-packing benchmark, it produced new best-known solutions for 10 of 12 targets (N=101-114), improving on prior records by 2.4%-5.4%, independently verified and accepted by Packomania, at a total LLM cost of $27.72 over 15 iterations in about 8 hours on a consumer PC. The authors also analyze cost-efficiency dynamics across iterations and propose a plateau-detection mechanism that would have cut cost by 50% with negligible quality loss, and catalog several non-trivial algorithmic ideas (e.g. hexagonal lattice initialization, defect migration) the LLM discovered on its own.
Applicable to our work
The core loop — LLM proposes a full candidate program, an independent zero-tolerance (no-shared-code) verifier checks it, only verified improvements are kept, with a scoreboard/history fed back into the next prompt — is structurally analogous to our best-of-N + frozen-verifier screening setup (problem 6, self-play/verifier-in-the-loop training) and to problem 1 (structural validity via execution/verifier-guided generation), though here the 'decoder' is an LLM prompted in natural language rather than our trained graph-transformer. The 'full replacement over patch' design choice and the per-target-best-tracking-separate-from-champion idea could inform how we structure rejection-sampled SFT rounds or best-of-N bookkeeping across many parts. The plateau-detection mechanism (windowed diminishing-returns stopping) is a directly transferable, cheap idea for deciding when to stop a best-of-N sampling budget or a self-play round for a given part/target.
Additional context
This is an LLM-agent (prompted-program-search) paper, not a trained neural decoder, so the applicability is at the level of loop design and verifier discipline rather than architecture. Results (circle packing record-breaking) are in a very different domain (continuous geometric optimization via classical numerical methods) and the 'verifier' here is a simple feasibility/overlap checker, much simpler than our scaled-Jacobian / surface-fidelity verifier — the core principle (independent, zero-tolerance, no-shared-code checker) is the transferable piece. Small-scale, single-author, non-peer-reviewed-venue paper (practicalsystems.io); treat headline numbers as self-reported though independently accepted by a third-party database (Packomania), which adds some credibility. The cost/iteration economics (specific dollar amounts) are not generalizable to our setting since they depend on LLM-as-agent pricing, not model training cost.

#2 0.093 HiFi-BRep is a two-stage VAE + latent-diffusion pipeline for B-Rep generation. The topo...
Contributions
The paper proposes HiFi-BRep, a framework addressing both representation and generation brittleness in B-Rep synthesis. It introduces a compact B-Rep representation (Bezier curves/surfaces plus an explicit edge-face incidence matrix) and a topology-aware encoder that eliminates padding noise via learnable queries and prevents feature contamination via topology-guided (Topo-Mask) attention, yielding a high-fidelity fixed-length latent. It also designs a single-stage decoder that jointly predicts geometry and topology in parallel and enforces the manifold 'two faces per edge' constraint via a differentiable row-wise two-peak objective, rather than deferring validity to post-processing. Experiments show state-of-the-art structural validity and geometric fidelity versus BRepGen, BrepDiff, and DTGBrepGen, plus faster inference.
Applicable to our work
Highly applicable to hexgen's B-rep encoder and structural-validity goals: (1) the Topo-Mask idea -- restricting cross-entity attention to only topologically adjacent face/edge pairs via a hard attention mask -- is directly transferable to hexgen's graph-transformer encoder over the B-rep face-adjacency graph, potentially sharpening pointer/copy-head addressing by preventing irrelevant cross-face attention; (2) the row-wise two-peak differentiable-validity-objective pattern (turning a hard combinatorial validity rule -- 'each edge has exactly 2 faces' -- into a supervised, differentiable training signal rather than a post-hoc filter) is a template for embedding some of hexgen's own frozen-verifier checks (e.g. simpler structural ones like feature-edge capture or element-budget sanity) directly into training rather than relying solely on best-of-N + reject; (3) their single-stage parallel joint geometry+topology decoding vs. cascaded generation ablation (Valid 95.2%->69.3% when reverting to cascaded) is direct evidence that jointly generating structure and parameters (as hexgen's pointer/coordinate-vocabulary decoder already does) outperforms sequential decomposition, reinforcing hexgen's existing single-decoder design choice.
Additional context
This is the closest paper of the four to hexgen's actual domain (CAD B-Rep generation) and the highest-confidence match. Code and pretrained models are public (github.com/1nnoh/HiFi-BRep), so architectural details (Topo-Mask implementation, row-wise two-peak loss) could be inspected directly rather than re-derived from the paper text. Note their B-reps are unconditional/class-conditional generative targets (~6-29 faces, similar scale to hexgen's 7-15 faces) rather than a program/DSL output, so this bears on the ENCODER and validity-loss side of hexgen, not the decoder's program-generation grammar.

#4 0.078 Defines a per-edge structural invariant, edge-girth g_e (length of the shortest cycle t...
Contributions
The paper proposes the per-edge pair (edge-girth, multiplicity) as an unbounded structural descriptor requiring no motif-size budget, and EGAGNN, a gated message-passing architecture that consumes it. On the Zinc-12k regression benchmark at matched ~100k parameters it reaches a test MAE a factor of 3 below the closest gated comparator and beats bounded cycle-count dictionaries unless they are extended to length 8. The authors also prove that on edge-girth-regular graphs the descriptor becomes constant and any model built on it collapses to the 1-WL bound, and verify this holds without exception on all 90 applicable pairs of the BREC isomorphism benchmark.
Applicable to our work
The edge-girth + multiplicity pair is a cheap, exact, unbounded-length structural edge feature that could be added to our graph-transformer encoder over the B-rep face-adjacency graph (7-15 faces, so BFS cost is negligible) to give edge-biased attention more topological signal without committing to a fixed cycle-length ceiling -- directly relevant to care area 4 (graph transformers / Graphormer-style edge-biased attention). It is also a candidate mechanism for helping the encoder disambiguate symmetric/orbit-congruent faces (another concern in care area 4), since edges lying on cycles of different lengths receive different values even when nodes look locally identical -- though the paper's own Proposition 1 shows it provides no help distinguishing graphs where every edge has the same edge-girth/multiplicity (e.g. highly symmetric block structures), which is worth checking against typical B-rep topologies before relying on it for symmetry-breaking.
Additional context
This is a small, rigorous, mostly negative-result-aware paper (a single independent-researcher-style academic paper) with released code and a formal expressivity proof, not just an empirical claim -- the kind of paper worth actually reading rather than skimming the abstract. The main risk for us is that B-rep face-adjacency graphs are small (7-15 faces) and may often be edge-girth-regular or close to it (e.g. simple block/box topologies), which is exactly the regime where this descriptor is proven to add nothing; a quick audit of edge-girth variance across our training corpus would tell us whether it's worth the (very low) implementation cost.

Contributions
The paper formulates executable video-editing planning and releases RefineCut-Bench, a benchmark with real clip/music metadata, explicit constraint ledgers, and multi-teacher trajectories. It proposes verifier-replayed trajectory distillation, which canonicalizes and replays noisy frontier-teacher branches through a deterministic verifier to produce verified SFT targets and preference pairs instead of imitating a teacher's unreliable first-choice branch. It introduces RefineCut-Evo, a verifier-centered, rubric-structured self-improvement stage trained with DPO on the student's own scored repair candidates. The authors show the resulting 8B open-weight planner substantially outperforms raw imitation, transfers across three backbones, and matches or exceeds its frontier API teachers when run in an identical closed verifier loop.
Applicable to our work
Strong structural analogy to hexgen's problem #1 (structural validity of generated programs) and #6 (verifier-in-the-loop self-improvement). The core transferable mechanism is 'verifier replay': instead of trusting the sampler's/teacher's first-choice output as a label, replay every candidate through the frozen, machine-checkable verifier and keep only verifier-best candidates as SFT targets -- directly applicable to screening hexgen's inverse-factory-sampled programs and best-of-N candidates before imitation training. Second, the two-stage recipe (SFT on verifier-best examples, then a self-improvement DPO stage where the student itself samples K candidates, gets them verifier+rubric scored, and trains on high-margin chosen/rejected pairs) is a ready template for a post-imitation self-improvement stage for hexgen, using its existing frozen verifier (compiles, zero inverted elements, quality bounds, feature capture, element-budget) as the scoring signal in place of RefineCut's constraint ledger. Third, the paper's decomposed branch-score (delta constraint-satisfaction, targeted repair, required-element recall, applicability, no-regression, locality) is a usable template for turning hexgen's pass/fail verifier checks into a graded scalar reward for ranking/repair. Fourth, their ablation showing verifier-only DPO scoring captures most of the gain (0.909 of 0.924 VES) with a learned/heuristic rubric adding only a small increment is encouraging evidence that a frozen-verifier-only self-improvement loop (no learned rubric) -- matching hexgen's current design -- can still deliver most of the benefit. Weaker, tangential borrow: the DAgger-adjacent framing of 'replay noisy demonstrations through an executor before imitating them' generalizes to hexgen's auto-labeled real-CAD programs from construction templates, which could analogously be verifier-replayed rather than trusted outright.
Additional context
Domain (video-editing planning) is unrelated to CAD/meshing, so nothing here transfers as a ready-made component -- the value is entirely in the training-recipe structure, which closely mirrors hexgen's own plan (imitation from a synthetic/auto-labeled sampler, screened best-of-N against a frozen verifier). The clean separation of 'verifier-only DPO' (0.909) vs. full rubric-margin DPO (0.924) is the most actionable data point: it suggests hexgen's planned frozen-verifier-only setup could capture most of a similar self-improvement gain without needing to build a learned quality rubric. Caveat: their verifier yields rich graded partial-credit (per-entry ledger pass rate), letting branch scoring be a smooth weighted sum; hexgen's verifier checks (compiles, zero inverted elements, Jacobian/aspect bounds, boundary fidelity, feature capture) may need to be combined into an analogous graded scalar (e.g., fraction of checks passed, or a small weighted sum) to replicate this branch-scoring mechanism rather than relying on a single binary pass/fail. Also note the paper explicitly flags that verifier-based metrics are in-protocol (not independent), a caution equally applicable to over-trusting a frozen verifier as the sole hexgen training signal without periodic human/held-out audits.

#6 0.075 A wall-first network reuses the Raster2Seq backbone (ResNet features + deformable-atten...
Contributions
The paper compares two ways of extracting vector geometry (walls, openings, rooms) from a single trained floorplan-vectorization network -- emitting the geometry as an autoregressive coordinate sequence versus detecting it from dense heatmaps and assembling a graph -- and shows which one wins depends on scan domain and plan size rather than being universally better. It also tests whether the room-centric versus wall-first output representation is what drives topological quality, and finds instead that a deterministic reconciliation/post-processing step and evaluation convention explain most of the difference, with the representation itself contributing mainly a coverage limitation. Building on this, the authors show that combining the two systems' outputs after generation (fusion) helps substantially, while feeding one system's output as an input prior to the other does not. They release an edit-cost evaluation metric and a new redistributable floorplan-vectorization benchmark (ResPlan-FP) along with corrected annotations for an existing dataset.
Applicable to our work
Several mechanisms are transferable by analogy even though the paper is 2D raster floorplan vectorization, not B-rep/CAD: (1) grammar/type-masked decoding that restricts the next token to what the output grammar allows at each step directly parallels our need for structural validity in the hexgen DSL decoder (e.g. masking to forbid referencing not-yet-emitted faces/points or duplicate IDs). (2) The training-free 'detect' readout -- reading structure from a dense/graph representation instead of full autoregressive emission -- is a useful design pattern for our pointer/copy addressing over the B-rep face graph, and their finding that autoregressive decoding degrades with output length while dense detection does not is a relevant caution for our program-length scaling. (3) Their deterministic post-hoc reconciliation/repair step (welding, dedup) that guarantees a validity property (no double walls) after generation is analogous to using our frozen verifier's checks as a deterministic repair pass rather than relying purely on generation-time validity. (4) Their closed-form condition for when combining two candidate outputs helps (donor purity > half the base F1) offers a reusable diagnostic for deciding when/how to fuse or select among best-of-N program candidates. (5) Their negative result on input-side conditioning (a deterministic re-encoding of the same evidence a model already sees adds no information, formalized via I(Y;D|X) <= H(D|X) = 0 for deterministic D) is a useful caution against expecting gains from feeding a decoder a second deterministic view of the same B-rep it already encodes, unless the added signal is genuinely new (e.g., stochastic search or execution feedback). (6) The edit-cost metric concept -- scoring drafts by repair effort rather than pass/fail -- could inspire an analogous 'verifier-fix-distance' metric for hexgen programs beyond binary structural validity.
Additional context
This is a single-author arXiv preprint (Aug 2026, not obviously peer reviewed) in an unrelated application domain (2D raster floorplan vectorization/CV), so nothing here is directly reusable as code or a component -- the value is purely as design-pattern inspiration. The two most transferable ideas are (a) grammar/type-masked autoregressive decoding for structural validity and (b) a training-free/lightweight 'detect' alternative to full autoregressive emission, both aligned with our top-priority structural-validity and pointer/graph-transformer problems. The negative input-side-conditioning result and the fusion purity condition are also worth keeping in mind when designing any candidate-combination or self-conditioning scheme for hexgen. Treat the applicability as analogical/tangential rather than a load-bearing citation.

The paper contributes SQL-Zero, an execution-verified self-play framework that trains a Text-to-SQL solver with zero annotated NL-SQL pairs, using only database schemas, contents, and execution as ground truth. Applicable to our work

The three-tier minimal reward (full credit for verifier-passing match, partial credit for 'compiles but fails verifier', zero for 'does not compile') is a simple, directly transferable reward-shaping pattern for any best-of-N or RL-style hexgen training signal. The paper proves that mixed training (synthetic and real data combined throughout SGD) induces strong model collapse -- a persistent excess-risk floor -- while two-stage training, which restricts synthetic data to an initial stage, avoids this floor entirely, showing collapse is not inevitable under a simple data curriculum. Contributions
The paper proposes SWYB, a lookahead-guided decoding framework for context-free grammars that augments local prefix-feasibility checks with bounded pushdown-automaton distance-to-acceptance estimates, enabling budget-aware pruning and beam search. Across JSON, SQL, and Linear Temporal Logic generation tasks and three base LLMs, SWYB is the only method that achieves 100% syntactic validity in every setting while also improving task-specific accuracy (schema validity, SQL execution accuracy, LTL semantic correctness) over prior constrained decoders, at a moderate decoding-time overhead.
Applicable to our work
Directly transferable to our hex-meshing DSL decoder's structural-validity problem: our DSL (block/sweep/projection commands with a closed coordinate vocabulary) is plausibly expressible as a context-free grammar, so compiling it to a PDA and using SWYB's offline bounded-distance-to-acceptance summaries plus online horizon-aware pruning/beam search could guarantee every generated program is syntactically well-formed (compiles) within budget, directly addressing care area #1 (structural validity, beam search over program prefixes) and reducing reliance on best-of-N rejection sampling purely for syntax. This is a strong, released-code (github.com/Enzossssss/SWYB), mechanism-level fit even though the evaluated domains (JSON/SQL/LTL) are unrelated to CAD.
Additional context

Contributions
The paper introduces Selective Regenerative Decoding (SRD), an inference-time decoding algorithm that performs segment-level intervention on reasoning trajectories instead of whole-trajectory accept/reject, regenerating only a trajectory's degraded suffix while keeping its useful prefix. It proves SRD achieves a provable 1.28-1.36x sample-efficiency gain and strictly higher expected best-trajectory quality than rejection sampling, with formal termination and monotonicity guarantees. Across MATH500, GPQA Diamond, HotpotQA, and AlpacaEval, SRD matches Best-of-N accuracy with substantially fewer generated tokens and beats speculative rejection in low-compute regimes.
Applicable to our work
Directly borrowable as a best-of-N alternative for hexgen's own program sampling: instead of discarding whole failed candidate programs from the frozen verifier, route partially-valid candidates (e.g. correct block/sweep prefix but a broken final projection command) to 'Refine' and regenerate only the tail of the program conditioned on the verifier's failure signal, keeping the valid prefix. This is a concrete mechanism for structural validity via repair/edit distillation (area #1) and best-of-N self-improvement flywheels (area #6) that doesn't require a larger teacher model -- a good fit since hexgen's verifier is frozen and machine-checkable, giving a clean segment-level signal (which command first breaks execution) to drive Keep/Refine/Discard routing.
Additional context
Domain is LLM text/math reasoning (token-level trajectories scored by a separate reward model), not structured DSL program generation, so the routing/reward machinery (rank-normalized thresholds tuned per task) would need re-deriving against our verifier's specific failure modes rather than a scalar reward-model score. The theoretical efficiency-gain guarantees rest on assumptions (e.g. stochastic dominance of refinement) that would need re-validating in the DSL-program setting, but the core three-phase Keep/Refine/Discard mechanism is architecture-agnostic and a natural fit for a frozen, machine-checkable verifier like ours.
