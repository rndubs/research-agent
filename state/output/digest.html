0.039 3D CAD consensus selection: sample N parametric CAD programs from an LLM, compile each...
Contributions
The paper proposes verifier-free 'consensus selection' for text-to-CAD generation: instead of using a separate vision-language judge, it compiles every sampled candidate program to a 3D model and picks the one that agrees most with the rest of the pool, under geometric or topological notions of agreement. On the candidate pools of a state-of-the-art CAD generation method, geometric consensus improves all three geometric metrics over that method's own verifier, and topological consensus matches it on topology. The method is training-free and works across every tested LLM and prompt variant, reducing Chamfer distance by 1-10% versus random selection from the same pool.
Applicable to our work
Directly applicable to hexgen's best-of-N candidate screening: alongside (or as a cheap pre-filter before) the frozen machine-checkable verifier, a consensus/agreement score computed across the compiled meshes of the N sampled hex-meshing programs could re-rank or triage candidates using signal the pool itself provides, potentially reducing how often the expensive verifier must run.
Additional context
Consensus selection implicitly assumes the majority of sampled candidates are 'roughly correct', so the informative signal is majority agreement; if hexgen's decoder occasionally produces a correct-but-structurally-distinct minority candidate (e.g. a valid but unusual block/sweep decomposition), consensus selection could systematically penalize good outliers -- worth checking against hexgen's actual candidate-pool diversity before adopting wholesale.

0.027 Introduces 'consilience', a verifier-free rollout-selection metric that scores each rea...
Contributions
The paper shows confidence-based verifier-free test-time scaling catastrophically breaks down on hard problems because uniformly high confidence often marks a failure to explore rather than correctness. It introduces consilience, a metric that evaluates the temporal asymmetry of a rollout's confidence trajectory (low-then-high) to pick better completions without any external verifier. Experiments on graduate math, QA, and free-form code generation show consilience beats confidence-maximization and matches or exceeds verifier-based selection in some settings. It notably works in domains like free-form code generation where voting-based selection is inapplicable.
Applicable to our work
Hexgen screens best-of-N candidate programs with an expensive frozen machine-checkable verifier. Consilience's confidence-trajectory signal could serve as a cheap, verifier-free pre-filter or complementary ranking over decoder rollouts -- e.g. to triage which of N sampled programs to run through the verifier first under a compute budget, or as an additional selection signal alongside the verifier's pass/fail result.
Additional context
Validated only on free-form LLM token-level reasoning (math/code/QA); hexgen's decoder emits short DSL programs via pointer/copy heads over a small closed vocabulary, a very different confidence regime, so it's unclear the same low-then-high confidence signature would emerge -- would need empirical validation on hexgen's own rollouts before relying on it to replace/triage the frozen verifier.

0.026 Hypothesis Frontier is a verifier-guided neurosymbolic search loop: each LLM-proposed f...
Contributions
The paper introduces Hypothesis Frontier, which evaluates every LLM-generated first-order formula exactly against training data, uses the concrete errors to drive bounded symbolic repair of the LLM's own formula, and retains the strongest verified formula across LLM rounds rather than treating each generation as final. It also proposes a final exact-simplification pass that shortens train-valid formulas without changing any training prediction, and a symbolic-first cascade that runs a Z3 solver before invoking the LLM loop at all. Under matched models and LLM-round budgets it beats repeated direct prompting in 9 of 9 comparisons.
Applicable to our work
The core loop — when a best-of-N candidate fails our frozen verifier, don't discard it; extract the concrete residual (which check failed: inverted elements, Jacobian/aspect-ratio bound, surface-fidelity mismatch, feature-edge miss) and use it to drive a bounded, parent-derived repair of the model's own DSL program (edit/patch the offending block/sweep/projection call rather than resample from scratch), then keep a deterministic 'best verified-so-far' frontier program across repair rounds and feed its residual failures back as context for the next generation attempt — this maps directly onto our structural-validity and repair/edit-distillation care area. The 'symbolic-first cascade' (run a cheap deterministic procedure first, only invoke the learned model on what it can't resolve) also echoes our existing deterministic-construction-template auto-labeling path and suggests we could similarly gate hexgen invocation behind template-based solvers for the parts they already cover.
Additional context
The domain (first-order logic formula induction with a trivial finite-model verifier) is far cheaper to check than our mesh verifier (compiling + meshing + Jacobian/aspect-ratio computation), so a per-round repair loop of this style would be much more expensive for us per iteration; the paper's own ablation shows repair reduces mismatch on 83-98% of invalid candidates but reaches exact validity immediately only 1-8% of the time, which is a useful expectation-setter for how much a similar repair loop might realistically buy us rather than a guarantee of large gains. 0.025 RA-CAD (ReAct Agent for CAD) runs a state-aware Generate-Execute-Critique-Rewrite loop:...
Contributions
The paper formulates state-aware text-to-CAD generation as a closed loop with post-execution critique modeled as an explicit, learnable agent-side policy component rather than a fixed prompt or post-hoc reviewer. It introduces trajectory-level Feedback-driven Agent Optimization (FAO) built on CAD Code Bootstrapping (CCB), which assigns terminal CAD-quality rewards (F1 + Chamfer Distance) to the entire Generate-Execute-Critique-Rewrite trajectory and jointly optimizes code generation, critique, and rewriting via GRPO. Experiments on CADFusion and Text2CAD show this closed-loop approach achieves state-of-the-art execution validity and geometric quality versus prior text-to-CAD methods and strong proprietary LLMs used as 8-shot baselines.
Applicable to our work
This is a close methodological match to hexgen's structural-validity problem (care area 1) and worth studying closely: (1) the Generate-Execute-Critique-Rewrite loop with an explicit, learned critique policy conditioned on execution feedback is a candidate mechanism for repairing hexgen's own broken best-of-N rollouts -- instead of (or in addition to) discarding candidates that fail the frozen verifier, a trained critique/rewrite step could target-fix them, directly relevant to the 'repair/edit distillation, DAgger-style learning to fix a model's own broken rollouts' line item. (2) The reward design -- hard-zeroing the geometric-fidelity reward whenever the candidate program fails to execute -- mirrors how our own frozen verifier's pass/fail gate could be composed with a continuous quality signal (scaled-Jacobian/aspect-ratio/surface-fidelity) for a GRPO-style trajectory-level RL stage on top of hexgen's current supervised-imitation training. (3) Trajectory-level (not per-step) reward assignment over an interaction that includes both generation and self-critique tokens is a concrete recipe if we want to add an RL fine-tuning stage after hexgen's current SFT-on-inverse-factory-data regime. (4) The ablation isolating the contribution of the critique component (removing it while keeping GRPO degrades performance) is useful evidence that a dedicated repair/critique head, not just outcome-reward RL, is worth the added complexity.
Additional context
Domain is parametric sketch-extrude CAD code (not B-rep/hex-meshing), and the base model is a general LLM (Llama-3-8B) rather than a small graph-transformer with pointer/copy heads, so architecture details do not transfer directly -- the transferable content is the training-loop pattern (execute -> critique -> rewrite -> trajectory-level RL with an execution-gated reward), not the network design. Baselines are 8-shot-prompted proprietary LLMs, which is a somewhat easy comparison; the more informative comparison for us is the CCB/FAO/Critique ablation in Table 3. Chamfer Distance and F1-over-topological-tokens are the fidelity metrics used; our own verifier's scaled-Jacobian/aspect-ratio/surface-match criteria would need their own reward shaping if adapting this recipe.

0.021 RISTER is an encoder-decoder STR network combining a rotation-equivariant encoder (RELG...
Contributions
The paper proves that the cross-attention mechanism is rotation-invariant when its query is held fixed and its source (key/value) tokens are rotated, and uses this to derive design principles for a Rotation-Invariant Text Decoder (RITD). It also proposes RELG, a rotation-equivariant local-global backbone combining deep equivariant convolutions with self-attention to preserve fine-grained detail and model inter-character dependencies. Combining RELG and RITD yields RISTER, an end-to-end rotation-invariant scene text recognizer that needs no orientation-estimation module or rotation-based data augmentation.
Applicable to our work
Our own 'make the encoder natively rotation-invariant' line is closed, but this paper's most transferable idea is decoder-side and architecture-agnostic: constructing the decoder's query purely from already-emitted output tokens (not from the raw, possibly-rotated input features) makes any subsequent cross-/pointer-attention over the input features provably invariant to whatever transformation was applied to those features. Applied to our pointer/copy decoder over B-rep faces, this suggests forming the pointer query solely from the previously-emitted program tokens (never mixing in raw per-face coordinate features into the query itself) so that the face-addressing/copy attention becomes provably rotation-invariant regardless of how the B-rep's face features are rotated — a decoder-only change that doesn't require revisiting the (closed) equivariant-encoder question. The underlying proof technique (cross-attention over an unordered token set is invariant to any joint transform of keys/values given an invariant query) generalizes beyond 2D images to graphs/sets in general.
Additional context
Because our 'encoder rotation-invariance' line is explicitly closed per the problem statement, most of this paper's direct architectural contribution (the RELG equivariant-convolution backbone, tied to a discrete C4 group over 2D pixel grids) has limited transfer value and does not obviously port to 3D SE(3) B-rep face graphs, which are non-grid and variable-cardinality. The decoder-side invariant-query trick is the one genuinely new, cheaply-testable mechanism, and it is worth flagging that RISTER's guarantee is exact only at 4 canonical angles and approximate elsewhere — our own rotation robustness needs are likely continuous (arbitrary part orientation), so the guarantee would only partially transfer.

0.009 The authors prove that for a given feature space and input symmetry group, the increase...
Contributions
The paper gives a rigorous mathematical characterization of 'symmetry increase' in equivariant neural networks: it proves the increased symmetry has a computable infimum fixed by the feature space, derives an orbit-type-based algorithm and design guidelines to reach that infimum, and shows under regularity assumptions that generic equivariant maps attain it. Experiments on synthetic k-fold-symmetric structures and the real-world QM9 dataset validate the theoretical predictions and show the guidelines reduce harmful representation collapse.
Applicable to our work
Our B-rep face graphs frequently contain congruent/symmetric substructures (e.g. repeated faces from patterned or symmetric parts), which is exactly the setting where pointer/copy heads face orbit/symmetry ambiguity when addressing faces. This paper's symmetry-infimum framework and feature-design guidelines are a genuinely new (not our closed rotation-invariant-encoder) mechanism for reasoning about and reducing harmful symmetry collapse in our graph-transformer encoder's features, which could directly inform how we design pointer/copy addressing to avoid ambiguous ties between congruent faces.
Additional context
Highly theoretical (group-representation-theory heavy); applying it to our setting would require translating the orbit-type analysis from continuous SO(3)/O(3) symmetry groups to our discrete/mixed graph-symmetry setting (permutation symmetry among congruent faces), which is nontrivial. Code released at the linked GitHub repo, which may help validate applicability faster than re-deriving the theory from scratch.

0.008 Verifier-as-Gatekeeper (VaG): a pre-commit, progressive trust hierarchy (Cold->Warm->Ho...
Contributions
The paper identifies and formalizes a 'capability-contamination tipping point' in self-evolving LLM agents, showing skill accumulation is non-monotone and that contamination is structurally irreversible because later skills are distilled conditioned on earlier (possibly defective) ones, so post-hoc rollback of a source skill cannot undo the flawed reasoning inherited by its descendants. It proposes VaG, a pre-commit gating mechanism combining three complementary per-skill critics with marginal-gain greedy subset selection, and demonstrates on Terminal-Bench 2 that VaG improves monotonically across five evolution rounds (reaching 72% pass@1 with a 5x smaller pool) versus ungated evolution's peak-then-collapse pattern, with the gated pool transferring positively across five backbones and to a second benchmark.
Applicable to our work
The core transferable mechanism is the pre-commit, multi-critic gating pattern applied to our own best-of-N verifier-screened candidate generation and any self-improvement/distillation loop we build around hexgen: (1) the 'irreversibility' argument -- that data/programs generated conditioned on a prior, possibly-flawed pool propagate contamination into later training rounds -- is directly relevant if we ever bootstrap new (B-rep, program) training pairs from the model's own verified outputs (rejection-sampled SFT / self-play flywheel), since a subtly-bad verified program could still bias later generations; this argues for pre-commit filtering of self-generated training data rather than only post-hoc pool cleanup. (2) The heterogeneous, cheap-first, conjunctive critic design (deterministic structural check -> behavioral replay -> expensive semantic/LLM check) is a reusable template for staging our own frozen verifier (compiles -> zero-inverted -> quality bounds -> feature-edge capture) to front-load the cheapest checks. (3) Marginal-gain greedy subset selection for catching combinatorial (not just individual) failure modes could inform how we curate a growing self-play training corpus, since two individually-valid synthesized programs could still be redundant or jointly harmful to training-set diversity/quality.
Additional context
This is an agent-skill-library paper (Terminal-Bench 2 CLI tasks), not CAD/mesh generation -- the bridge to hexgen is purely at the level of the self-improvement/verifier-gating methodology, not the domain. The 'structural validity' critic here (SchemaCritic) is a much weaker notion (frontmatter schema check) than our frozen geometric verifier, so don't over-read the terminology overlap. Worth citing as evidence for why any future self-play/rejection-sampling loop for hexgen should gate admission of new synthetic training examples before they influence subsequent generations, not just filter at generation time.

Contributions
The paper argues CAD evaluation must test engineering behavior, not just visual/geometric correctness, and introduces CADEngBench to do so. Additional context
This is a benchmark/evaluation paper, not a method paper -- it doesn't propose a generation technique to borrow, but it's a strong candidate source of evaluation methodology (B-Rep validity + FEA-matched checks + parameter perturbations) that hexgen's own verifier design could be compared against or extended with.

#1 0.058 Models the per-instance false-accept rate as a latent variable drawn from a distributio...
Contributions
The paper gives a minimal theory of partially correlated verifier cascades: modeling per-instance false-accept/true-accept rates as latent variables, it derives that posterior log-odds are concave in the number of gates k (with the independence-based Odds Law as just the tangent/upper bound), that failure can decay only polynomially instead of exponentially under correlation, and that a 'blind-spot' probability mass caps achievable reliability below 1 regardless of how many gates are added. It also shows the theory is empirically measurable and that independence-based extrapolation can underestimate failure by orders of magnitude at larger k.
Applicable to our work
Directly applicable to designing/calibrating hexgen's own frozen verifier, which is itself a multi-gate cascade (compiles; zero inverted elements; scaled-Jacobian/aspect-ratio bounds; surface-match fidelity; feature-edge capture; element-budget sanity): this theory gives a principled way to estimate how correlated those gates are, whether adding more checks is still buying reliability or hitting a blind-spot ceiling, and suggests decorrelating checks (e.g. across independent geometric criteria) rather than stacking more of the same kind of check when screening best-of-N candidates.
Additional context
This is a theoretical/methods note (builds on arXiv:2606.15712, the 'Odds Law' paper) rather than an applied CAD or program-synthesis paper; abstract-only extraction. The practical takeaway ('decorrelate, don't just add gates') is the most directly actionable piece for hexgen's verifier design.

Contributions
The paper proposes VRR-Stop, a robust stopping framework for noisy verify-repair-repeat loops in LLM agents that models verifier and repairer noise separately and decides whether to continue repairing based on the sign of the estimated marginal gain in validity. It also introduces VRR-Guard, an estimation-free fallback for when verifier discrimination is too weak for calibration to be reliable. On a GSM8K stress setting, VRR-Stop substantially improves final true validity over fixed-round repair while using fewer average repair rounds.
Applicable to our work
Directly transferable stopping/decision framework for hexgen's best-of-N plus frozen-verifier plus repair pipeline: a VRR-Stop-style noise model and belief-filtering could decide when to keep repairing a candidate hex-mesh program versus commit or discard it, and VRR-Guard's margin-based fallback guards against acting on an unreliable signal, directly targeting the structural-validity and verifier-in-the-loop-training care areas.
Additional context
hexgen's verifier is described as a FROZEN, machine-checkable, deterministic pass/fail (compiles, zero inverted elements, quality bounds, etc.), so it likely has much higher discrimination than the noisy LLM verifiers this paper targets -- the explicit noise model may be unnecessary overhead, but the general stopping-criterion framing (marginal-gain sign, cost-bounded repair, guard against low-signal regimes) remains a directly applicable design pattern for a repair/edit-distillation loop.
